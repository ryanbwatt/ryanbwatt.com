I spent a few weeks deciding what kind of business I want to build. The
answer was transformation: go into a small business, connect its tools into
one working layer, teach the owner to run it, and refresh how the business
presents itself. No product on day one. The first job is to prove the work
adds value.

## Who is already doing it

Five companies shaped the idea.

<img src="/logos/longlake.svg" width="16" height="16" alt="" /> [Long Lake](https://llmh.com/)
buys US services businesses, architecture firms among them, and runs them all
on one shared AI platform it calls Nexus. One named layer, reused everywhere.

<img src="/logos/percepta.png" width="16" height="16" alt="" /> [Percepta](https://www.percepta.ai/)
embeds engineers inside large companies and runs on its own orchestration
platform. Hands-on delivery, backed by a platform and playbooks.

<img src="/logos/titan.png" width="16" height="16" alt="" /> [Titan](https://titanmsp.ai/)
buys IT managed service providers and rebuilds them around AI. It makes an
existing services business AI-native instead of selling it software.

<img src="/logos/crescendo.png" width="16" height="16" alt="" /> [Crescendo](https://www.crescendo.ai/)
sells customer service as a platform plus the team that runs it, live in under
30 days. An outcome with a fixed time to value.

<img src="/logos/ap.png" width="16" height="16" alt="" /> [Applied Poetics](https://ap.ltd/)
is a small New York agency for technology, image and design, working with
fashion brands, architects and studios. One small team can carry both the
technology and the taste.

Three of the five are General Catalyst bets, and all of them work at
enterprise or acquisition scale. The small-business version is still open.

## The model

- One niche to start: galleries, architects and design studios.
- One fixed-scope engagement: a command center for the business, training for
  the owner, and a brand refresh.
- The first clients at little or no fee, in exchange for publishing the
  results.
- What repeats becomes a named program with a fixed fee and a monthly
  retainer.
- Reputation first, then network, then income. Real revenue is a three-to-five
  year goal.

## Being found

Small business owners now ask AI assistants who can help them. To be the
answer:

- Own a narrow question ("an AI command center for a gallery"), not a broad one
  ("AI consultant").
- Give the layer its own name. Nexus is taken.
- Publish the plain explainer of what a small-business command center is, and
  case studies with real numbers.
- Be cited elsewhere: podcasts, directories, forums, press. Assistants trust
  other sources more than your own site.
- Keep the site plain and fast, with structured data and an `llms.txt` file.
- Every month, ask the assistants the questions a client would ask and note who
  they name.

## What it became

The first proof is [Gallery Assistant](https://gallery-assistant.pages.dev/),
a command center for an art gallery. Inventory, collector inquiries and sales
sit in one place. A matching layer pairs each work with the collectors most
likely to want it and drafts one-to-one outreach for review. Nothing is sent
without approval. The demo runs on fictional data.
