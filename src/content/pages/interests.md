Things I'm interested in:

- **What AI is doing to writing.** Models favour the em dash because it's
  everywhere in the journalism, books, and academic papers they trained on,
  and because repeated punctuation is under-penalised during training. The
  interesting part isn't the tell, it's the direction: AI-generated text
  strips filler, brevity and concision are what get rewarded, and the more of
  that we read and imitate, the more voice gets cannibalised across all
  writing. Lee McIntyre's *Post-Truth* argues we can get trapped in echo
  chambers where the same ideas are reiterated back to us. If we increasingly
  start our thinking by prompting rather than sketching or arguing, how much
  of our thinking ends up being the model's? Em dashes will get trained out.
  What won't, because it's less visible?

- **Customer success as a revenue function.** Above $50m ARR, most new revenue
  comes from existing customers rather than new logos — and most org charts
  still put the biggest team, the best comp, and the CEO's attention on the
  new logo. Expansion is treated as a side quest. Why does the structure lag
  the economics by so far, and what does an org look like that doesn't?

- **Adoption as the thing that decides everything.** An account that isn't
  using its second and third core features by the end of onboarding almost
  never picks them up later; by month six the workflow has hardened, around
  your product or around the workaround. Which makes onboarding the highest
  leverage window there is, and it's usually staffed with the newest people
  and measured by time-to-launch.

- **Looking at art.** I studied art history before any of this, and it's
  training in staying with something until you notice what's actually there
  rather than what you expected. It's the same faculty that reading an account
  well requires, and I've never seen it taught anywhere in business.

- **Running.** Four marathon majors so far — Chicago 2:41, Boston 2:46, New
  York 2:53, Berlin 2:53. The endurance question I keep circling: how much of
  a ceiling is physiological and how much is a belief you haven't tested?
